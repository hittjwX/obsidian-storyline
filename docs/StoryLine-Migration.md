# How To Migrate Your Book Into StoryLine

## Overview

- From a bunch of notes to a clean manuscript is critical for authors who want to get published.
- How to digest your book idea into a writing workflow that stays organized and leverages StoryLine features.
- The purpose of this document is to get your works form idea, writing, editing, and be ready to publish.
- This document will cover some technical aspects when import isn't straight forward. Help writing process.

## Compatibility With Writing Tools

- Scrivener. Import via the StoryLine plugin for Obsidian or open natively with Storyline Scribe.
- Obsidian. Start with the StoryLine plugin for Obsidian. 
- Microsoft Word
- Google Docs
- Joplin
- Typora
- Ulysses
- Zettlr. If you are using Zettlr with Obsidian, the fastest migration path is to open the Zettlr repo as an Obsidian vault, then use the StoryLine plugin.

## Questions to Answer Before You Start


- Is your book draft or notes linear? If your book is in a single document, that is linear. You can import it into StoryLine, then digest into scenes.
- Is your book a mess of notes and drafts? Before your import into StoryLine, clearly label notes and chapters. Import first the chapters, assigning them to Acts. Then assign only the relevant notes.
  - At this point, extract from the notes to write out and make progress on your book. If you dump notes into the environment that's movement not progress.
  - The Kanban or Plotgrid is the skeleton of your book. Placing notes where they belong helps write rather than shovel papers.
- What beat sheet or style do you want to follow? It's okay if you don't know this when you begin. However, knowing you'll use a "Hero's Journey" or "3-Act Structure" helps build out the skeleton of your book.
  - If you don't know what structure, focus on building individual scenes and placing them in a sequence. Within scenes you might have chapter order. 
  - It is more important to get your body of work into the writing environment than the exact structure at this point. Focus on capturing key ideas during migration.
- Where were you storing parts of your book before? It's okay to bring together shoeboxes, journals, short stories, and other materials into a note-taking application.
  - It's always better to capture materials in a scene, or note associated with a scene, then to have your book shotgun around your writing space.
